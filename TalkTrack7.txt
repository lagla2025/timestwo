## SLIDE 1 — COVER (~20 sec)

This is how people used to find each other.

Personals. Classifieds. Want ads.

Clear, direct, a little awkward. But it worked.

That was then.

---

## SLIDE 2 — ME (~40 sec)

This is what it looks like now.

A photo. A few lines. A version of yourself designed to be chosen.

And everything that follows depends on that first impression.

I spent 20 years building audiences through content. National Geographic, Discovery, MTV, Ancestry.

The insight behind Times Two isn't technical. It's behavioural.

What you read, watch, listen to, and play. Decompressing. Digesting. Discovering. None of it performative.

Times Two matches people based on the content they already have in common.

---

## SLIDE 3 — THE PROBLEM (~50 sec)

And this isn't working.

Match Group went from 48 billion to 7. Bumble from 8 billion to under 500 million.

One and a half million paying users gone in a single year.

This isn't a discovery problem. People are matching. It's an introduction problem. There's no common ground. So the conversation stalls or dies.

"Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." Charlie Munger said that. *(MUNG-er, rhymes with hunger)*

---

## SLIDE 4 — THE SOLUTION (~1 min)

Meet Times Two.

Instead of showing you a profile, it shows you the overlap. Based on what you actually read, listen to, and engage with.

You see it in small moments. She didn't scroll past the Middle Management piece. She listens to The Daily every morning before she's ready to talk to anyone. He invited her to Crossplay. She said yes before she knew who he was.

That's not something you write about yourself. That's a pattern.

In early interviews, when people saw their own content behaviour reflected back, the reaction was consistent.

They didn't ask "do I like this person?"

*(pause)*

They asked "what would I say to them?"

The conversation is easier because the common ground was already there. Something real starts. And they don't leave where it happened.

Same audience. Wrong mechanism. Soulmates was still built on profiles and bios. When free apps arrived, paying 32 pounds a month for the same mechanic became indefensible.

Soulmates never used the data. We do.

## DEEP BENCH Q&A (back pocket, don't say unless asked)

*The distilled 12-card Q&A is in QA_Final.txt. Below is the full depth for hard follow-ups.*

**"Your research subjects are on academic licenses, not paying subscribers. Why would paying users behave the same way?"**

We tested with people who engage with the content daily. Paying subscribers engage more deeply because they chose to be there. The behaviour is the same. The commitment level is higher for paying users, not lower. The academic license gave us access to the behaviour. The paywall is what makes the pool valuable. One proves the other.

**"Who are your competitors?"**

Hinge, Bumble, Tinder: performed identity, broken business model. Raya: celebrity-gated. The League: LinkedIn-verified, career filter. Facebook Dating: 3 billion users, all the data, nobody talks about it. Data without trust is noise. Spotify on Hinge: decorative, still self-presentation. Guardian Soulmates ran for 16 years, 500K members, 15M pounds revenue. Right audience. Wrong mechanism. Killed by free apps. Nobody is using publisher behavioural data as the matching engine. That is the white space.

**"What's stopping Hinge or Bumble from just doing what you do?"**

Their revenue model depends on the mechanism that is damaging their users. Intermittent reinforcement. Decision fatigue. Performance anxiety. They are throwing AI at it. But AI applied to a broken mechanism is still a broken mechanism. You cannot algorithmically fix a product whose revenue depends on the problem it is supposed to solve. Times Two sidesteps the entire trap. The matching happens inside an ecosystem people already trust and already pay for.

**"Has this been tried before? What is the precedent?"**

Guardian Soulmates. Launched 2004. 500,000 members from Guardian readership alone. 80% joined because of the brand. 15M pounds revenue. Failed because it was still profile-based. Same mechanism as Tinder. Data breach in 2017 damaged trust. Times Two owns the trust and safety stack entirely. That liability never touches the publisher. The licensing model is the same: we build the engine, the publisher keeps the brand.

**"What about Facebook Dating?"**

Free. More personal data than any platform on earth. Nobody talks about it. Data without trust is noise. Nobody wants their romantic life adjacent to their aunt's holiday photos. Times Two sits inside a publisher ecosystem people already trust and already pay for.

**"What about the SWOT?"**

Strengths: first mover in publisher-embedded dating, behavioural data nobody else has, built-in trust from publisher brand, direct precedent in Soulmates. Weaknesses: solo founder, no technical co-founder yet, dependent on publisher partnerships. Opportunities: market in decline with no credible replacement, publishers seeking new revenue lines, Crossplay proves willingness. Threats: publisher says no or takes too long, privacy regulation tightens, users find content signals creepy.

**"How does the AI/Wingman work technically?"**

API customer. We call the model, pass the data, get the brief, display it. The AI provider never sees who the user is. Our moat is the data pipeline, the system prompt, the product logic, and the match algorithm. None of that lives at the AI provider.

**"What if NYT says no?"**

NYT is an accelerant, not a prerequisite. Guardian first. Soulmates precedent, experimental culture, no paywall barrier. This is a sequencing story, not a single point of failure.

**"Walk me through the Year 3 revenue."**

20,000 users. 30% convert to paid at $8/month: $576K user subs. Publisher licensing $2-3/active user/month across all 20,000: $480-720K. Venue partnerships and events: $200-400K. Total: $1.5-2M. Assumes one to two publishers.

**"How did you arrive at a $5M pre-money valuation?"**

Comparable seed-stage B2B SaaS valuations. 20 years domain expertise. Size of addressable market. Defensibility of matching methodology. Working prototype. 16 qualitative interviews. Active subscriber study. $5M is the middle of $3-8M range for pre-seed/seed B2B SaaS.

**"How much do publishers pay for the infrastructure?"**

$2-3 per active user per month. Publisher charges end user $8-15/month. Keeps the difference. 60-75% margin on a product they did not build.

**"What if a publisher just acquires Times Two?"**

Acquisition is a valid exit. $15-30M price floor at Year 2-3. But selling to one publisher caps upside because they would kill multi-publisher model. Buying us and locking it down makes their own product worse.

**"How does the subscriber actually get Times Two?"**

Three packaging options. Option A: Standalone add-on at $8/month. Option B: Bundled into All Access, publisher pays us $2-3/user. Option C: Freemium, free to browse content overlap, pay for the reveal and chat. Publisher chooses. Flexibility is a feature.

**"Why can't NYT just do this themselves?"**

They don't build new categories. They buy proof. The Athletic, Wordle, Wirecutter. We're building the proof. Also: NYT has a Not Invented Here problem. We walk in with mechanism and evidence, not users. The pitch is: license us, run a pilot, if it doesn't reduce churn, turn it off. Downside risk to the publisher is close to zero.

**"Are you pitching two products? Dating and friendship?"**

No. One product. Dating. 62% of survey respondents want friendship. That's future upside, not current scope. Same engine, new use case. Like Bumble to BFF.

**"What if only one publisher says yes?"**

Guardian alone: 175K eligible singles, $2M/year at maturity. NYT alone: 1.7M singles, $10M+. First publisher proves the model. Second proves the network effect.

**"What about church and state?"**

Times Two is a commercial product. Same shelf as Cooking, Games, Athletic, Wirecutter. The data we use is the same data NYT already monetizes through Perspective Targeting for ad sales. Same data. New application. Explicit consent.

**"What about chat safety?"**

NYT already moderates 12,000 comments a day. The paywall is the first filter. Bots don't pay $25/month. We build and operate all trust and safety. Publisher licenses the product. We own the liability.

**"What about pricing and freemium?"**

$8/month standalone. Free tier shows content overlap but not who. No photo. No name. No chat. Paid tier is the reveal. Content first. That's the product philosophy built into the pricing.

**"What's a SaaS multiple?"**

Recurring subscription revenue valued as a multiple of annual revenue. $2M at 10x = $20M valuation. 8-10x is standard for seed-stage B2B SaaS. Conservative and credible at our stage.

**"What about existing publisher dating sites?"**

Publisher dating has existed for 22 years. All standalone websites. No content integration. No subscriber data. Publisher logo on the door and nothing else. White Label Dating's parent collapsed in 2024. The old model is breaking. Times Two is embedded, behavioural, subscription-gated. Same licensing model. Completely different product.

**"What about the publisher problem? Why would they do this?"**

Publishers have three unsolved problems. Passive engagement: people are already interacting with content socially but nothing deepens it. Churn: content is replaceable, the relationship isn't. Unused data: the richest behavioural dataset in media, not being used for the subscriber. Times Two connects all three.

**"What about the competition in detail?"**

Hinge, Bumble, Tinder: present yourself, get judged, repeat. Raya and The League filter for status. Facebook has the data but not the trust. The Guardian proved the audience exists but used a traditional profile-based model. Nobody is using publisher behavioural data as the matching engine. White Label Dating powered 6,500 sites before its parent collapsed. 70+ publisher dating sites still running. All standalone. All profile-based. No data integration. Times Two is the first to embed inside the publisher ecosystem and match on behaviour.

**"How does affinity become connection?"**

Content consumed passively. Affinity signal detected. Perceived similarity surfaced. Introduction made. Real conversation starts because common ground already existed. Subscriber stays because something real happened. Behavioural science: Wolf and Tomasello 2020 on shared experience bonding. Byrne 1971 on similarity-attraction. Montoya 2008 meta-analysis confirming perceived similarity as strongest predictor. We create the conditions. We don't guarantee the outcome.

**"Is the matching technology feasible? Does the data infrastructure exist?"**

NYT already built the AI matching engine. They call it BrandMatch. It analyses a brand's message from a client brief and uses generative AI to match it with the most contextually relevant content and audience segments across the NYT portfolio. Campaigns using BrandMatch see +30% click-through and 30% higher video completion rates. That's the same matching logic as Times Two, just pointed at advertisers instead of people. We're proposing the same technology matched to subscribers instead of brands. The infrastructure exists. The data exists. We're proposing a new application of it. (Source: The Drum, March 2026, interview with Tusar Barik, SVP Marketing, NYT Advertising.)
